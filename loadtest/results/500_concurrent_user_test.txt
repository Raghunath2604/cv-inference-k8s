Real load test at the exact concurrency the resume claims: 500 concurrent
users, single unscaled instance, 1 vCPU sandbox.

Scaling progression (1/10/50/100/500 users), each a separate 10-30s run:

Users | Median  | p99    | Throughput | Failures
------|---------|--------|------------|----------
1     | 8ms     | 31ms   | 5 req/s    | 0%
10    | 7ms     | 45ms   | 57 req/s   | 0%
50    | 43ms    | 260ms  | 212 req/s  | 0%
100   | 270ms   | 590ms  | 212 req/s  | 0%
500   | ~1900ms | ~3300ms| ~190 req/s | 0%

Interpretation: zero failures at every concurrency level (the service
degrades gracefully under overload rather than crashing or dropping
connections). Throughput plateaus at ~210 req/s starting at 50 users and
never exceeds that regardless of additional load — the signature of a
single CPU core running at capacity and queueing the rest, not a code
bug. Confirmed this progression is smooth and monotonic rather than
checking only the 500-user endpoint in isolation.

Honest conclusion: the resume's "sub-100ms latency during traffic spikes"
claim describes the intended behavior of the FULL system with Kubernetes
HPA actually spreading 500 concurrent users across up to 10 pods on real
multi-core infrastructure (see k8s/hpa.yaml). It does not describe — and
should not be read as describing — a single replica's behavior, which is
what was measured here. That composite claim (500 users + sub-100ms,
simultaneously, via working autoscaling) remains unverified end-to-end,
because doing so requires a real multi-node cluster this sandbox cannot
reach (see README's Docker/Kubernetes section for why).
